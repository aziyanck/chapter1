TOPIC 7: ANALOG DATA TO ANALOG SIGNAL
======================================================================
Analog Modulation: AM, FM & PM

Key Concepts:
  - Analog-to-analog conversion: Transmitting analog information using an analog carrier signal
  - AM: Carrier amplitude varies with the message; modulation index m = A_m / A_c
  - FM: Instantaneous frequency varies with the message; β = Δf / f_m
  - PM: Carrier phase varies with the message
  - AM → Amplitude, FM → Frequency, PM → Phase

--- 1. Analog-to-Analog Conversion  (Definition & Types) ---
Definition: Analog-to-analog conversion is the process of transmitting analog information using an analog carrier signal.
Key Points:
  - It is also called analog modulation.
  - The figure shows the two inputs: the message (the information) and the carrier (a plain high-frequency wave that carries no information on its own).
  - The modulator combines them into the modulated signal (AM, FM or PM), shown on the next slides.
The Three Main Techniques:
  - AM — Amplitude Modulation
  - FM — Frequency Modulation
  - PM — Phase Modulation
Example: In conventional AM radio broadcasting, the amplitude of a carrier signal varies according to the audio signal.

--- 2. Amplitude Modulation (AM)  (Carrier Amplitude Follows the Message) ---
Definition: AM is a modulation technique in which the amplitude of the carrier signal varies according to the instantaneous amplitude of the message signal.
Working:
  1. Message input — The message signal, such as audio, is supplied to the modulator.
  2. Modulation — The modulator varies the carrier amplitude according to the message.
  3. Transmission — The modulated signal is transmitted through an antenna.
  4. Demodulation — The receiver demodulates the signal to recover the original audio.
Important Point:
  - In conventional AM, the carrier frequency remains constant while its amplitude varies.
Formula:
Modulation Index:
  m = A_m / A_c
  where: m = modulation index; A_m = peak amplitude of the modulating signal; A_c = amplitude of the unmodulated carrier
  Note: For a single-tone message and conventional AM, m is normally kept at or below 1 to avoid overmodulation.
Advantages:
  - Simple to generate and demodulate.
  - Can be implemented using relatively simple receivers.
Disadvantages:
  - More sensitive to amplitude noise.
  - Conventional AM can be power-inefficient.

--- 3. Frequency Modulation (FM)  (Carrier Frequency Follows the Message) ---
Definition: FM is a modulation technique in which the instantaneous frequency of the carrier varies according to the message signal.
Working:
  1. Message input — The message signal is supplied to the modulator.
  2. Modulation — The carrier frequency increases or decreases according to the message.
  3. Transmission — The modulated signal is transmitted.
  4. Demodulation — The receiver detects the frequency variations and recovers the original message.
Important Point:
  - In ideal FM, the carrier amplitude remains constant while its instantaneous frequency varies.
Formula:
FM Modulation Index:
  β = Δf / f_m
  where: β = FM modulation index; Δf = peak frequency deviation; f_m = modulating frequency
Advantages:
  - Better resistance to amplitude noise than conventional AM.
  - Can provide good audio quality.
Disadvantages:
  - Often requires more bandwidth than conventional AM.
  - The transmitter and receiver can be more complex.

--- 4. Phase Modulation (PM)  (Carrier Phase Follows the Message) ---
Definition: PM is a modulation technique in which the phase of the carrier signal varies according to the instantaneous amplitude of the message signal.
Working:
  1. Message input — The message signal is supplied to the modulator.
  2. Modulation — The modulator changes the carrier phase according to the message.
  3. Transmission — The modulated signal is transmitted.
  4. Demodulation — The receiver detects the phase variations to recover the message.
Important Point:
  - In PM, the carrier's phase varies according to the message signal. Its instantaneous frequency also varies when the message phase changes over time.
Advantages:
  - Can provide good resistance to amplitude noise.
  - Forms an important foundation for digital phase modulation.
Disadvantages:
  - Requires suitable phase-modulation and demodulation circuits.
  - The resulting frequency variations depend on how the message changes.

Formulas Summary:
  AM Modulation Index: m = A_m / A_c
    (Peak message amplitude divided by unmodulated carrier amplitude; kept at or below 1.)
  FM Modulation Index: β = Δf / f_m
    (Peak frequency deviation divided by modulating frequency.)

Exam Tips:
  - Explain AM, FM and PM, and compare them.
  - Define modulation index and frequency deviation.
