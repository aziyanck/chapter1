# 3. Propagation Modes

## 1. Definition of Propagation Modes

**Propagation modes are the different ways electromagnetic waves travel from a transmitting antenna to a receiving antenna.**

Radio waves can travel along the Earth's surface, through the atmosphere, or directly between antennas.

The main propagation modes are:
1. Ground-wave propagation
2. Sky-wave propagation
3. Line-of-sight (LOS) propagation

---

## 2. Ground-Wave Propagation

**Definition:** Ground-wave propagation is the transmission of radio waves along the Earth's surface.

### Working
- Radio waves travel close to and follow the Earth's surface.
- The waves can bend around the Earth's curvature to some extent.
- Propagation depends on frequency and ground conductivity.

**Frequency:** Particularly useful at lower frequencies, such as LF and MF.

**Applications:** AM broadcasting at suitable frequencies, maritime communication and navigation systems.

**Advantages:**
- Can cover beyond the visual horizon.
- Useful for communication over the Earth's surface.

**Disadvantages:**
- Signal strength decreases with distance.
- Ground absorption can weaken the signal.
- Less suitable for high-frequency transmission over long distances.

---

## 3. Sky-Wave Propagation

**Definition:** Sky-wave propagation is the transmission of radio waves that travel toward the ionosphere and are refracted back toward the Earth.

### Working
1. The transmitting antenna sends radio waves upward.
2. The waves enter the ionosphere, a region of the upper atmosphere containing charged particles.
3. Under suitable conditions, the waves are refracted back toward Earth.
4. The signals can travel long distances, sometimes through multiple hops.

**Frequency:** Commonly associated with the HF band (3–30 MHz).

**Applications:** Shortwave broadcasting, amateur radio and long-distance radio communication.

**Advantages:**
- Enables communication over very long distances.
- Can reach beyond the line of sight.

**Disadvantages:**
- Depends on ionospheric conditions.
- Signal strength and quality may vary.
- Communication can be affected by solar activity and atmospheric changes.

---

## 4. Line-of-Sight (LOS) Propagation

**Definition:** Line-of-sight propagation occurs when radio waves travel along a direct path between the transmitting and receiving antennas.

### Working
- The transmitting antenna sends radio waves toward the receiver.
- The receiver must be within a suitable radio path, with sufficient clearance from obstacles.
- The Earth's curvature limits the distance between antennas near the ground.

**Frequency:** Commonly used for VHF, UHF and microwave communication.

**Applications:** Television broadcasting, mobile communication, Wi-Fi links, microwave links and satellite communication.

**Advantages:**
- Supports high data rates with suitable equipment.
- Provides relatively predictable propagation when the path is clear.
- Suitable for directional communication links.

**Disadvantages:**
- Buildings, hills and other obstacles can block or weaken signals.
- Coverage is limited by the radio horizon.
- Often requires suitable antenna placement.

---

## 5. Space-Wave Propagation

**Definition:** Space-wave propagation is the transmission of radio waves through the space between transmitting and receiving antennas, generally involving a direct wave and sometimes a ground-reflected wave.

It is commonly used in terrestrial radio communication.

### Types of space waves
- **Direct wave:** Travels directly from the transmitting antenna to the receiving antenna.
- **Ground-reflected wave:** Reaches the receiver after reflecting from the Earth's surface.

**Applications:** Television broadcasting, radar and microwave communication.

**Important:** Space-wave propagation and line-of-sight propagation are closely related concepts. In many introductory textbooks, LOS propagation is discussed as part of space-wave propagation.

---

## 6. Tropospheric Propagation

**Definition:** Tropospheric propagation involves radio waves travelling through the troposphere, the lowest layer of Earth's atmosphere.

### Types
- **Tropospheric scatter:** Signals are scattered by irregularities in the troposphere, allowing communication beyond the normal horizon.
- **Tropospheric ducting:** Atmospheric conditions can trap radio waves in a layer, allowing them to travel unusually long distances.

**Applications:** Specialized long-distance VHF and UHF communication.

---

## 7. Ionospheric Propagation

**Definition:** Ionospheric propagation involves the interaction of radio waves with the ionosphere, a region of the upper atmosphere containing charged particles.

The ionosphere affects radio waves differently depending on their frequency and the atmospheric conditions.

**Applications:** Shortwave radio communication and some navigation systems.

**Important:** Sky-wave propagation is the main propagation mode associated with ionospheric refraction.

---

## 8. Radio Horizon

**Definition:** The radio horizon is the maximum distance over which two antennas can communicate through a direct radio path, considering Earth's curvature and atmospheric refraction.

The radio horizon is often slightly farther than the geometric horizon because of atmospheric refraction.

For practical terrestrial links, antenna height is an important factor in determining the radio horizon.

---

## 9. Optical Horizon

**Definition:** The optical horizon is the maximum distance at which an object can be seen along a direct visual path, considering Earth's curvature.

**Difference:** The radio horizon can extend beyond the optical horizon because radio waves may be refracted by the atmosphere.

---

## 10. Factors Affecting Propagation Modes

1. **Frequency:** Different frequencies behave differently during propagation.
2. **Distance:** Greater distances generally cause greater signal loss.
3. **Atmospheric conditions:** Temperature, humidity and ionospheric conditions can affect radio waves.
4. **Obstacles:** Buildings, mountains and trees may block or weaken signals.
5. **Ground conductivity:** Affects ground-wave propagation.
6. **Antenna height:** Influences line-of-sight coverage.
7. **Transmitted power:** Affects the received signal strength.
8. **Interference:** Other signals can affect communication quality.
