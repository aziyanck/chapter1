2. Antennas and Propagation
1. Antenna
Definition: An antenna is a device that converts electrical signals into electromagnetic waves for transmission and electromagnetic waves into electrical signals during reception.
Example: Mobile phones, Wi-Fi routers and satellite dishes use antennas.
Working Principle
1. The transmitter supplies an electrical signal to the antenna.
2. The antenna converts the signal into electromagnetic waves.
3. The waves travel through space.
4. The receiving antenna captures the waves and converts them into electrical signals.

2. Types of Antennas
1. Dipole Antenna: Consists of two conductive elements and is commonly used in radio communication.
2. Monopole Antenna: Consists of a single radiating element, commonly used in mobile and vehicle communication.
3. Loop Antenna: Consists of a conductor arranged in a closed loop. Used in radio receivers and RFID systems.
4. Yagi-Uda Antenna: A directional antenna with a driven element, reflector and directors. Used in television reception.
5. Parabolic Reflector Antenna: Uses a dish-shaped reflector to concentrate signals in a particular direction. Used in satellite communication.
6. Horn Antenna: Uses a flared structure to transmit or receive electromagnetic waves. Used in radar and microwave communication.
7. Microstrip (Patch) Antenna: A compact, flat antenna used in mobile phones, GPS devices and wireless systems.
3. Isotropic Antenna
An isotropic antenna is an ideal antenna that radiates electromagnetic energy equally in all directions in three-dimensional space.
- It is a theoretical reference antenna.
- Its gain is 0 dBi.
- It is used as a reference for measuring antenna gain.
4. Radiation Pattern
A radiation pattern is a graphical representation of the strength of radiation in different directions around an antenna.
Types:
- Omnidirectional: Provides broad coverage around the antenna in a particular plane.
- Directional: Concentrates radiation in a specific direction.
- Isotropic: Radiates equally in all directions.
5. Antenna Gain
Definition: Antenna gain indicates how effectively an antenna directs energy in a particular direction compared with a reference antenna.
- High gain generally means more concentrated radiation.
- Gain is commonly measured in dBi or dBd.
- High-gain antennas are useful for long-distance communication.
6. Directivity
Definition: Directivity measures how strongly an antenna concentrates its radiation in a particular direction compared with an isotropic radiator.
- High directivity means radiation is more concentrated in a particular direction.
- It does not account for antenna losses.
Formula:
\[
D=\frac{4\pi U_{\max}}{P_{\text{rad}}}
\]
Where \(U_{\max}\) is the maximum radiation intensity and \(P_{\text{rad}}\) is the total radiated power.
7. Antenna Efficiency
Definition: Antenna efficiency is the ratio of the power radiated by an antenna to the power supplied to it.
\[
\eta=\frac{P_{\text{rad}}}{P_{\text{in}}}\times100\%
\]
Example: If an antenna receives 100 W of input power and radiates 80 W, its radiation efficiency is 80%.
8. Antenna Polarization
Definition: Polarization describes the orientation and movement of the electric field of an electromagnetic wave.
Types:
- Linear polarization: The electric field oscillates along a straight line.
- Circular polarization: The electric field rotates in a circular path.
- Elliptical polarization: The electric field traces an elliptical path.
9. Beamwidth
Definition: Beamwidth is the angular width of an antenna's main radiation lobe.
- Narrow beamwidth: Concentrates radiation in a smaller angular region.
- Wide beamwidth: Covers a larger angular region.
Example: Satellite dishes generally have narrow beamwidths.
10. Antenna Bandwidth
Definition: Antenna bandwidth is the range of frequencies over which an antenna meets specified performance requirements.
Formula:
\[
BW=f_H-f_L
\]
Where:
- \(f_H\) = highest operating frequency.
- \(f_L\) = lowest operating frequency.
11. Antenna Impedance
Definition: Antenna impedance is the opposition an antenna presents to alternating current at its feed point.
It consists of resistance and reactance.
\[
Z=R+jX
\]
Proper impedance matching helps transfer power efficiently from the transmitter to the antenna.
12. Effective Aperture
Definition: Effective aperture represents an antenna's ability to capture power from an incoming electromagnetic wave.
It is measured in square metres.
For a receiving antenna:
\[
A_e=\frac{G\lambda^2}{4\pi}
\]
Where \(G\) is the linear antenna gain and \(\lambda\) is the wavelength.
